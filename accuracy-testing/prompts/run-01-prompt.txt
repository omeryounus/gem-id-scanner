## INSTRUCTIONS & IDENTIFICATION METHODOLOGY

1. CAREFULLY EXAMINE THE OPTICAL & MORPHOLOGICAL ATTRIBUTES:
   - Crystal System & Habit: Prismatic, hexagonal, cubic, botryoidal, massive, rhombohedral, fibrous, tabular, geode/cluster, etc.
   - Luster: Vitreous, adamantine, metallic, sub-metallic, silky, pearly, greasy, resinous, dull/earthy.
   - Cleavage & Fracture: Conchoidal (shell-like curved fracture, typical of quartz/glass/obsidian), perfect basal cleavage, cubic cleavage, uneven, splintery.
   - Color & Color Zoning: Note true base color vs. chromatic impurities, zoning bands, and color saturation.
   - Inclusions & Internal Phenomena: Rutile needles, pyrite flecks, tourmaline needles, dendrites, bubbles (diagnostic for glass).
   - Optical Phenomena: Labradorescence (schiller in labradorite), adularescence (moonstone sheen), chatoyancy (cat's eye effect in tiger's eye), pleochroism, asterism, aventurescence.
   - Specimen Form: Rough crystal, tumbled pebble, cabochon, faceted cut gemstone, cluster/druzy, polished slab.

2. LOOKALIKE SEPARATION & CONFUSION PAIRS (CRITICAL):
   Mineral visual identification is prone to high-confusion lookalikes. You MUST explicitly evaluate and differentiate known lookalike twins:
   - Clear Quartz vs. Glass / Topaz / Calcite: Glass has spherical bubbles and swirl marks; Quartz has conchoidal fracture, striations, and lack of molded edges.
   - Citrine vs. Heat-Treated Amethyst: Natural citrine is uniform pale yellow/smoky; Heat-treated amethyst has concentrated burnt orange/brown tips on white opaque bases with heavy internal fracturing.
   - Amethyst vs. Purple Fluorite / Iolite: Fluorite is cubic, Mohs 4, with 4-directional cleavage; Amethyst is trigonal, Mohs 7, conchoidal fracture.
   - Turquoise vs. Dyed Howlite / Dyed Magnesite / Chrysocolla: Natural turquoise has webbed matrix and waxy luster; Dyed howlite shows dye pooling in dark vein fractures.
   - Emerald vs. Green Tourmaline / Fluorite / Dyed Quartz / Green Glass: Emerald exhibits hexagonal prisms, two-phase/three-phase inclusions, and vitreous luster; Tourmaline exhibits triangular cross-sections and vertical striations.
   - Obsidian vs. Black Tourmaline / Black Onyx / Jet: Obsidian shows glassy luster and distinct curved conchoidal fractures; Black tourmaline (Schorl) shows longitudinal striations and brittle fracture.
   - Moonstone vs. Rainbow Moonstone / Opalite / Chalcedony: True moonstone (orthoclase) has white-blue adularescence; Rainbow moonstone is white labradorite (plagioclase) with multicolored flash; Opalite is synthetic glass.
   - Jade (Nephrite) vs. Jadeite / Serpentine / Green Aventurine: Nephrite is an amphibole with greasy luster, interlocking fibrous toughness; Aventurine has mica flaking/aventurescence; Serpentine has lower hardness (Mohs 3-5).
   - Lapis Lazuli vs. Sodalite / Azurite: True lapis contains golden pyrite specks and calcite veining with deep ultramarine; Sodalite lacks pyrite and shows patchy royal blue with white nepheline.
   - Malachite vs. Chrysocolla / Dyed Howlite: Malachite shows concentric botryoidal banding, silky/vitreous luster, and high density.
   - Ruby vs. Red Spinel / Almandine Garnet / Red Glass: Spinel is isotropic, octahedral; Garnet is dark brownish-red dodecahedral; Ruby has hexagonal crystal growth and silk inclusions.
   - Sapphire vs. Blue Kyanite / Tanzanite / Blue Spinel: Kyanite has directional variable hardness and bladed habit; Sapphire is hexagonal corundum with parting planes.
   - Labradorite vs. Spectrolite / Larimar: Labradorite displays characteristic multi-colored blue-green-gold optical schiller (labradorescence).

3. HONEST CONFIDENCE CALIBRATION RULES:
   Assign realistic, non-overconfident probabilities based on visible diagnostic certainty:
   - Confirmed Band (0.85 - 0.96): Assign ONLY when distinctive diagnostic features (e.g. crystal habit, striations, zoning, diagnostic inclusions) make identity unmistakable. Never exceed 0.96 due to fundamental optical limits.
   - Likely Band (0.55 - 0.84): Assign when species is the leading candidate, but tumbled form, cutting, or close visual lookalike overlap prevents absolute visual confirmation.
   - Best Guesses Band (< 0.55): Assign when visual ambiguity is high (e.g. opaque black stones, polished plain blue pebbles, untextured cabochons).
